L9: # Title:Evaluating and Benchmarking the System One Model Jev
L10: 
L11: Authors:【5†Tobias Deußer】, 【6†Lorenz Sparrenberg】, 【7†Rafet Sifa】

L14: 
L15: 【8†View PDF】 【9†HTML (experimental)】
L16: > Abstract:Jev is a commercial System One model from TypeSafe AI that does not generate text: given a state and typed questions, it returns a choice from fixed options, a position on a rubric, or the probability that a statement is true, with probabilities the vendor describes as calibrated. Such models target small decisions in information access pipelines, such as routing queries, checking grounding, moderating content, or rating against a rubric.
L17: We evaluate Jev (jev-1.13.0) zero-shot on 37 datasets spanning classification, routing, natural language inference, reading comprehension, commonsense reasoning, moderation, legal clause analysis and rubric scoring, with one frozen template per dataset and full evaluation splits: 346,009 requests for under USD 10. For reference, we score Qwen3.8-27B and Gemma-4-E4B on identical requests via their exact next-token probabilities over the options.
L18: Jev reaches 95-99% accuracy on IMDB, SST-2, HellaSwag and ARC and 86.7% on Belebele across 122 languages. It beats Qwen on 27 of 37 datasets, with none of Qwen's nine leads outside the bootstrap intervals, and Gemma on all 37. All three models degrade on low-resource languages, fine-grained or noisy labels, and rubric-based quality judgments. Jev's choice probabilities are well calibrated and support selective prediction.
L19: Binary probabilities rank well but are poorly placed relative to a fixed 0.5 threshold; thresholds tuned on training data raise micro-F1 on UNFAIR-ToS from 0.50 to 0.75. Jev answers MMLU's calculation-heavy questions more accurately than other MMLU questions (94% vs. 91%), whereas both open models, and all three on C-Eval, find them harder.
L20: Rotating the options leaves Jev's accuracy unchanged and withholding the question drops it to near chance, ruling out shallow memorization but not memorized question-answer pairs. We release the code, harness and all raw responses.

L9: # Title:Visual Jev: Accurate and Efficient Decisions from Shared Visual Context
L10: 
L11: Authors:【5†Guanxu Yu】, 【6†Yuhang Yao】
L12: 
L13: View a PDF of the paper titled Visual Jev: Accurate and Efficient Decisions from Shared Visual Context, by Guanxu Yu and 1 other authors
L14: 
L15: 【7†View PDF】 【8†HTML (experimental)】
L16: > Abstract:Many vision applications ask several independent, forced-choice questions about the same image. Visual Jev encodes the image and public context once, executes isolated question suffixes as a batch, and reads candidate probabilities from the backbone's language-model head. Across four benchmarks, answer-supervised post-training raises equal-weight macro accuracy from 70.6% to 76.1%, with the gain concentrated on the two task families represented in training.
L17: At N=32 questions per image, shared batched execution is 8.9x faster in warm amortized time than independent serial execution and remains 3.4x faster than an already-batched baseline that recomputes the prefix, at the cost of higher peak memory. A matched typed-head control offers no consistent accuracy advantage over the language-model-head readout. The supported design is therefore simple: adapt the backbone for quality, retain the existing readout, and share execution for efficiency.
